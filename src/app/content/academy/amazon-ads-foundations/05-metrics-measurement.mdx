## Amazon's Official Concept

Amazon Advertising provides the following core performance metrics:

**Impressions** — How many times your ad was displayed.

**Clicks** — How many times shoppers clicked your ad.

**Click-Through Rate (CTR)** — `Clicks ÷ Impressions`. Measures how compelling your ad creative is relative to competing placements.

**Cost Per Click (CPC)** — `Spend ÷ Clicks`. The average amount paid per click in the auction.

**Spend** — Total advertising dollars consumed in the selected period.

**Orders** — Total units purchased that are attributed to your ad click (within the attribution window, typically 7–14 days).

**Sales (Ad Revenue)** — Total revenue attributed to ad-driven orders.

**Conversion Rate (CVR)** — `Orders ÷ Clicks`. The percentage of ad clicks that result in a purchase.

**Advertising Cost of Sale (ACoS)** — `Spend ÷ Ad Revenue`. The percentage of ad-driven revenue consumed by advertising spend.

**Return on Ad Spend (RoAS)** — `Ad Revenue ÷ Spend`. Inverse of ACoS. RoAS of 4.0 = $4 revenue for every $1 spent.

**Total Advertising Cost of Sale (TACoS)** — `Spend ÷ Total Revenue (Organic + Paid)`. Measures advertising spend as a percentage of your entire business revenue, not just paid revenue.

**New-to-Brand (NTB)** — Orders from customers who have not purchased from your brand in the past 12 months. Measures customer acquisition contribution.

## Coach Wesley's Operator Layer

**The metric hierarchy — what matters at what level:**

Most sellers stare at ACoS. Operators look at ACoS last — after checking the metrics that *explain* why ACoS is what it is.

```
Impressions → CTR → Clicks → CVR → Orders → Revenue → ACoS
                                                         ↑
                                              You are here (most sellers)
```

The operator reads this left to right:
1. **Low impressions** → keyword bids too low, budget exhausted, or campaign not winning auctions. Solution: raise bids or budget.
2. **High impressions, low CTR** → main image, price, or review count not competitive. Solution: listing work, not bid work.
3. **High CTR, low CVR** → customers click but don't buy. Listing description, bullets, A+, or price competitiveness issue. Solution: listing conversion optimization.
4. **Acceptable CVR, high ACoS** → likely paying too much per click for the CVR you're getting. Check CPC vs Max CPC formula from Lesson 4.

**The TACoS vs ACoS distinction — why it matters:**

| Metric | What it measures | When to act |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|
| ACoS | Paid campaign efficiency | When paid CVR or CPC moves outside target range |
| TACoS | Ad dependency on total business | When rising without intentional spend scaling — investigate organic rank |

TACoS rising while ACoS stays flat = your organic sales are declining (organic rank is slipping). Ads are compensating for lost organic traffic, increasing your advertising dependence.

TACoS falling while ACoS rises = your organic sales are growing faster than your ad spend. This is often a positive signal — organic momentum is building.

**Benchmark thresholds by category (as starting references — not universal targets):**

| Category | Target ACoS range | Healthy CVR | Healthy CTR |
|---------|------------------|------------|------------|
| Supplements / Health | 20–35% | 8–15% | 0.3–0.6% |
| Electronics accessories | 15–25% | 10–18% | 0.4–0.8% |
| Home & Kitchen | 20–30% | 6–12% | 0.3–0.5% |
| Beauty / Personal care | 25–40% | 8–14% | 0.4–0.7% |
| Apparel | 30–50% | 3–8% | 0.3–0.5% |

These are reference ranges. Your target ACoS must be derived from your **profit margin**, not from industry benchmarks. A product with 65% gross margin can sustain 45% ACoS profitably. A product with 20% gross margin must maintain sub-15% ACoS to remain margin-positive.

**Break-even ACoS formula:**
```
Break-Even ACoS = Gross Margin % 
(i.e., if your gross margin is 40%, your break-even ACoS is 40%)
```

**Target ACoS** should be set below break-even to generate net contribution:
```
Target ACoS = Break-Even ACoS × (1 - Desired Net Contribution %)
Example: 40% margin, targeting 10% net contribution margin from ads
Target ACoS = 40% × (1 - 0.25) = 30%
```

## Scenario Snapshot

> **Account situation:** A campaign's ACoS improved from 42% to 31% over 30 days without any bid changes. The account owner is excited and considers scaling the budget. But TACoS went from 12% to 18% over the same period.

**Coach Wesley's call:** This is a classic ACoS improvement trap. ACoS improved because organic revenue declined — paid sales now represent a larger share of total revenue. If organic sales fell 25% while paid efficiency improved, the total business got weaker. Investigate organic keyword rank and review velocity before declaring this a win and scaling spend.

## Operator Takeaway

> **ACoS is the final output of a funnel: impressions → clicks → orders. Before you move ACoS, diagnose which stage of the funnel is broken.**

The three operators I've seen consistently outperform are the ones who read search term reports and placement reports before touching a single bid. Metrics are diagnostic instruments. They tell you *where* the problem is. The bid change is the *last* action, not the first.
