## Amazon's Official Concept

Amazon Advertising is a retail media network — one of the largest in the world. When brands advertise on Amazon, ads appear across the shopping journey: on search results pages, product detail pages, streaming services (Fire TV, Prime Video), and third-party websites via Amazon DSP.

Amazon classifies its advertising products into three primary categories:

**Sponsored Ads** — keyword and ASIN-targeted ads that appear within Amazon's shopping results. Billed on a cost-per-click (CPC) basis.

- Sponsored Products
- Sponsored Brands
- Sponsored Display

**Amazon DSP** — demand-side platform that enables programmatic display and video advertising across Amazon properties and third-party exchanges. Typically impression-based (CPM).

**Streaming TV Ads** — video placements on Prime Video, Fire TV, and Twitch.

The advertising auction is a **second-price auction**: you pay $0.01 above the next highest bidder, not your full bid. This is why bidding higher than your target doesn't necessarily mean you'll pay your full bid — but it does mean you'll win more auctions.

## Coach Wesley's Operator Layer

Here's what Amazon's training material won't emphasize enough:

**Ads don't sell products. Listings convert ads into sales.**

This is the most important mental model shift in retail media. A Sponsored Products ad is a traffic delivery mechanism. The listing is the sales machine. If your listing can't convert organic traffic, paid traffic will fail at a higher cost.

Before evaluating any ad strategy, operators run the **Retail Readiness Check**:

| Signal                | Minimum Threshold                                           | Why It Matters                                                  |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Review count          | ≥ 15 reviews                                                | Social proof is required for cold traffic to convert            |
| Star rating           | ≥ 4.0 stars                                                 | Below 4.0, conversion rate collapses regardless of ad placement |
| Main image            | Professional, white background, product fills 85%+ of frame | CTR is determined almost entirely by the main image             |
| Price competitiveness | Within 15% of top 3 competitors                             | Price gap kills conversion on high-intent keywords              |
| Buy Box               | Winning ≥ 90% of the time                                   | Ads that don't win the Buy Box don't convert                    |
| A+ Content            | Uploaded (if brand registered)                              | Increases CVR by an average of 3–10%                            |
| In-stock status       | > 30 days of inventory                                      | Never run ads into a stockout                                   |

**The operator rule**: Run the Retail Readiness Check _before_ any campaign goes live. If more than two signals are red, fix the listing. Every dollar you spend on ads before fixing conversion rate is a dollar you'll never get back.

## Scenario Snapshot

> **Account situation:** A 3P seller just relaunched a product after a month-long stockout. The listing is back in stock with 9 reviews (4.2 stars). Their previous campaign had decent ACoS (34%) before the stockout. They want to turn it all back on immediately.

**Coach Wesley's call:** Wait. 9 reviews is below the 15-review threshold where cold traffic conversion becomes reliable. The campaign previously performed well because it had 34 reviews at the time. Now the social proof stack is smaller and click-through-rate will be lower. Resume at reduced budget — test search-term efficiency for 2 weeks — then scale once CVR data confirms the listing is converting at ≥ 8% again.

---

## Operator Takeaway

> **Ads buy traffic. Listings convert traffic. Fix conversion rate first — always.**

The most efficient ad account I've ever seen was one with zero fancy bid strategies and a listing with 4.8 stars, 900 reviews, and a 14% conversion rate. The second most efficient was the same listing with a professional video. Neither was about the bidding. It was about the conversion machine underneath the traffic.

Amazon's advertising console has 40+ optimization levers. The highest-ROI lever is a listing rewrite or a new main image. That's the one operators check first.
