## Amazon's Official Concept

Sponsored Products targeting falls into two primary modes:

**Automatic Targeting** — Amazon's algorithm selects which search terms and product pages to show your ad on, based on your product's detail page content, category, and historical performance signals. Amazon divides auto targeting into four sub-strategies:
- **Close match**: Queries closely related to your product
- **Loose match**: Broader, related queries
- **Substitutes**: Product pages of similar items (your competitors)
- **Complements**: Product pages that often pair with yours

**Manual Targeting** — You specify the exact keywords or ASINs you want to target.

*Keyword targeting* lets you bid on search terms. Match types (broad, phrase, exact) determine how narrowly Amazon interprets your targets.

*Product (ASIN) targeting* lets you target specific competitor ASINs or product categories directly — your ad appears on their product detail page.

**Negative targeting** blocks specific search terms or ASINs from triggering your ads:
- **Negative exact**: Blocks a precise search query
- **Negative phrase**: Blocks any query containing that phrase
- **Negative ASIN targeting**: Prevents your ad from appearing on a specific product page

## Coach Wesley's Operator Layer

**The strategic role of each targeting type:**

**Auto targeting** is your intelligence-gathering operation. Its job is to expose which queries Amazon believes are relevant to your product. Many of these will be wrong. Some will be goldmines you'd never have thought to target manually. You run auto at a controlled budget and harvest its search term report weekly.

**Exact targeting** is your revenue engine. These are proven, high-intent queries where you've confirmed purchase intent and margin. You put your largest bids here. Exact campaigns should receive the majority of your budget over time.

**Broad and phrase** targeting sit between discovery and scale — useful for testing keyword variations, catching long-tail queries, and building negative lists.

**ASIN/product targeting** is a competitor conquest and defensive strategy:
- Offensive use: Target competitor ASINs that are weaker than yours (lower reviews, higher price, worse imagery). Your ad appears on their page when a customer is already in buying mode.
- Defensive use: Run self-targeting campaigns on your own ASINs so competitors can't conquest your page without you fighting back.

**The negative keyword discipline operators maintain:**

Running a campaign for 2 weeks without adding negatives is burning money. The search term report will always contain irrelevant queries consuming spend. Operators add negatives **weekly at minimum**.

Common bleeders to negative:
- Branded competitor names (unless you're explicitly running a conquest strategy)
- "Free" + anything (never converts)
- Irrelevant size/color variations not in your catalog
- Questions ("how to," "what is," "where to buy" on a product selling only on Amazon)
- Low-price anchoring terms ("cheap," "discount," "under $10") if your product is premium-priced

**The auto targeting on/off switch most sellers miss:**

Amazon auto targeting can be individually enabled or disabled by sub-strategy. If you notice that "loose match" is burning 40% of your auto budget with 0 conversions but "close match" has a 12% CVR — you can **turn off loose match** and let close match run with the full budget. This is one of the highest-ROI optimizations available in a new campaign.

## Scenario Snapshot

> **Account situation:** An auto campaign has been running for 3 weeks. You pull the search term report and find 22 search terms with >$10 spend and zero conversions. The top bleeder: "amazon fire tablet" showing up for a Kindle case ASIN. $87 spent. Zero orders.

**Coach Wesley's call:** "Amazon fire tablet" is an irrelevant query — customers searching for a tablet are not buying a Kindle case. Add as negative exact immediately. Run through the full list: any term with >$15 spend and zero conversions that is clearly irrelevant gets negative exact applied today. Terms with spend but no conversions that are *relevant* get a longer look — they may need more data or a lower bid, not a negative.

## Operator Takeaway

> **Auto campaigns are search detectives. The weekly search term report is their report back to you. Never let the report sit unread for more than 7 days.**

The sellers who lose money on Amazon PPC are almost always the ones who set up auto campaigns and check them monthly. Weekly negative keyword maintenance is the single highest-ROI habit in paid advertising on Amazon. It takes 20 minutes. It compounds.
