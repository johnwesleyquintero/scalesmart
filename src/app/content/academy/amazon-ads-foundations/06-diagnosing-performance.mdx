## Amazon's Official Concept

Amazon provides the following report types for campaign analysis:

**Search Term Report** — Shows every search query that triggered your ad, with spend, clicks, orders, and ACoS per term. Available at campaign/ad group level for Sponsored Products.

**Placement Report** — Shows performance broken down by placement: Top of Search, Rest of Search, Product Pages. Essential for evaluating placement multiplier effectiveness.

**Targeting Report** — Performance by keyword or ASIN target. Useful for isolating which specific keyword bids are over or underperforming.

**Advertised Product Report** — Performance broken down by ASIN. Identifies which specific products are driving (or hurting) campaign efficiency.

**Campaign Performance Report** — High-level campaign metrics aggregated over time.

Amazon recommends reviewing these reports regularly and using them to adjust bids, budgets, and targeting based on performance trends.

## Coach Wesley's Operator Layer

Amazon's guidance is correct but abstract. Here is the concrete protocol operators use when an account underperforms:

### The 7-Step Diagnostic Protocol

**Step 1: Observe**
Pull TACoS and ACoS for the trailing 7, 14, and 30 days. Note direction (rising, falling, stable) and velocity (fast change vs slow drift). **Do not act yet.**

**Step 2: Segment**
Break performance down by:

- Campaign type (auto vs exact vs brand)
- Match type
- ASIN / product
- Placement (TOS vs ROS vs product pages)

Ask: Is the problem in _one_ campaign or across all campaigns? Is it isolated to _one_ ASIN or all products? This is the most time-saving step — it narrows 80% of possible causes in 5 minutes.

**Step 3: Isolate**
Using your segment data, identify the specific variable that changed:

- Did CPC increase? (Check search term report for expensive new terms)
- Did CVR drop? (Check listing reviews, price, Buy Box %, stock level)
- Did impressions spike? (Auto campaign expanded into irrelevant queries)
- Did a specific ASIN's performance collapse? (Check ASIN-specific listing health)

**Step 4: Hypothesize**
Form a specific, testable hypothesis:

> _"Auto campaign is bleeding $240/week on 'kids toys' queries for an adult supplement product. Negating these terms will reduce spend by ~$200/week with minimal revenue impact."_

Never move to action without a written hypothesis. It forces clarity and creates an audit trail.

**Step 5: Act**
Make the single smallest change that tests your hypothesis. Do not stack multiple changes simultaneously — you'll lose the ability to attribute cause and effect.

Common interventions by root cause:

| Root cause identified          | Intervention                                          |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Irrelevant search terms        | Add negative exact targets                            |
| High CPC on low-CVR terms      | Bid reduction on specific keywords                    |
| Low CVR on all keywords        | Listing audit: reviews, images, price                 |
| Budget exhausting by 10am      | Increase budget or reduce bids to extend delivery     |
| Specific ASIN dragging account | Separate it into its own campaign; isolate its P&L    |
| Placement inefficiency         | Adjust placement multiplier based on placement report |

**Step 6: Monitor**
Set a review date. For bid changes: 7 days minimum before evaluating. For structural changes: 14–21 days. For budget changes: 3–5 days. **Resist the urge to check daily and panic-adjust.** Amazon's ad delivery algorithm needs time to recalibrate.

**Step 7: Verify**
Did the intervention do what you predicted? Compare pre- and post-change metrics for the specific variable you changed. Document the result. This builds your internal knowledge base on what works for this specific account, category, and product profile.

---

**The Bleeder Identification Protocol** (search term audit):

Open the Search Term Report. Filter by date range: last 30 days. Sort by Spend (descending).

Flag any search term meeting **any** of these criteria:

- Spend > $20 AND Orders = 0 AND query is clearly irrelevant → **Negative Exact immediately**
- Spend > $50 AND Orders = 0 AND query may be relevant → **Hold for 7 more days, then negative if still no order**
- Spend > $30 AND ACoS > 2× target ACoS → **Bid reduction of 20–30%**
- Spend < $5 AND Orders > 1 → **Harvest into manual exact campaign with 10–15% bid increase**

This exercise, done weekly for 30 minutes, is how accounts move from 45% ACoS to 28% ACoS over 90 days — without finding a magic optimization lever.

## Scenario Snapshot

> **Account situation:** You take over a $15,000/month ad account. ACoS is 52%. The previous manager blamed "increased competition." You have 30 minutes to identify the primary issue.

**Coach Wesley's call:**

Step 1 — Segment by campaign type. The Auto campaign is 61% of spend with 71% ACoS. The two exact campaigns are at 34% and 29% ACoS (healthy). Problem isolated to the Auto campaign.

Step 2 — Open the Auto search term report. Filter: Spend > $30, Orders = 0. Result: 38 search terms. Top 5 together: $1,840 spend, 0 orders. These are the bleeders.

Hypothesis: Adding 38 negatives will reduce Auto spend by ~$1,800/month and reduce overall account ACoS from 52% to ~39% within 30 days.

Action: Add all 38 as negative exact to the Auto campaign. Set a 14-day review date.

The previous manager blamed competition. The real cause was 38 search terms the algorithm had been matching for months with zero conversion signal.

## Operator Takeaway

> **The 7-Step Protocol is not a framework — it's a habit. The operators who consistently outperform are the ones who diagnose before they act, every single time.**

The biggest mistake in Amazon advertising is treating it like a mechanical system: "ACoS high → lower bids." Accounts are organic, competitive, and constantly changing. The protocol keeps your analysis disciplined when the data gets noisy and the client is panicking.

Write the hypothesis. Run the minimum viable intervention. Monitor. Verify. Document. Repeat.

That's the job.
